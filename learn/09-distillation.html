Chapter 9

Distillation and "healing"

After pruning, the smaller model can be retrained to imitate the original. Done properly that needs a GPU cluster. What fits on 16 GB is a local, layer-by-layer version, and in our runs it did not help.

Try it: hard labels versus the teacher's distribution

Training on plain text only rewards the one token that appeared (cross-entropy with the hard label goes to 0 when the student puts 100% on "␣is"). Distillation (KL to the teacher) is lowest when the student copies the teacher's whole distribution, including "␣is" being only 62% likely and "," a real alternative. That extra information is why distillation recovers a pruned model faster than plain training. Teacher numbers are illustrative.

What to remember

Knowledge distillation: the original model is the teacher, the pruned model the student. Both read the same text; the student is trained to minimise the KL divergence between its next-token distribution and the teacher's. It learns from every probability, not just from the one token that appeared.

Real distillation backpropagates through all 40 layers and keeps optimizer state for every parameter, about 16 bytes per parameter, so roughly 300 GB for a 19 B student. NVIDIA recommends 80 to 100 B tokens of it for this level of compression.

06_heal.py does what fits: one layer at a time, it trains only the router rows so that the pruned MoE block reproduces the original block's output (mean squared error on hidden states). The local error went down, but perplexity got slightly worse at both K=48 and K=128. Matching hidden states locally is a different goal from predicting the next token well.

Then the real thing (step 3, appendix F). With the loss on the model's output and its gradient streamed back through all 40 layers, one layer on the GPU at a time, the routers were trained for real: the KL divergence to the teacher fell by 18%. But held-out perplexity improved only 0.6% (5.92 → 5.88) and the benchmarks stayed within noise. Routers can only choose among the experts that survived; the knowledge in the deleted ones needs trainable experts and far more data.
