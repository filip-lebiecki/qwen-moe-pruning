Chapter 1

Tokens and hidden states

A language model never sees letters. Text is cut into tokens, each token becomes a row of 2048 numbers, and the whole model is a machine that edits those numbers until they predict the next token.

Try it: real Qwen3.5 tokenizations

The token pieces and IDs are exactly what the model's tokenizer produces. The colored strips are a stand-in for the embedding row (32 of the 2048 numbers, colored by sign and size). Notice that a repeated token such as 2 always gets the same row: the embedding table is a lookup, not a calculation.

What to remember

Tokens are word pieces. Common words are one token; rare words, other languages and numbers are split into several (Polish text above needs 11 tokens for 3 words, and every digit of a number is its own token).

Embedding: each token ID picks one row of a 248,320 x 2048 table. That row is the token's starting hidden state. The 40 layers then repeatedly edit these vectors, and the lm_head turns the last vector into one score per vocabulary token. The probabilities in the animation are real: they are what your pruned 19B model predicts after "The capital of Australia".

Parameters are all the numbers stored in these matrices. In BF16 each one takes 2 bytes, which is why 35 B parameters are about 67 GiB on disk.
