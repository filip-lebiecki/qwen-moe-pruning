What to remember

    
The 48-expert model still writes fluent, well-formatted text, and simple arithmetic works, because the reasoning
      experts were well represented in calibration. What breaks first is knowledge and structure: it cannot produce working
      code, and on a factual question it falls into a repetition loop, a typical sign of a damaged model. The same
      failure appears in the BF16 file, so it comes from pruning, not from 4-bit quantization.

    
The 128-expert model answers all three correctly. Its explanations can still be simplified or slightly off: with
      thinking on, it said Sydney was rejected for being "too close to the coast and favored by New South Wales", while the
      main reason was the Sydney–Melbourne rivalry. Always test real prompts in your own domain before trusting a pruned model.

    
A third 8.9 B model, calibrated on code only (run 3), shows the other side. It passed every Python unit test, but
      asked the capital question it answered: "The capital of Australia is A. It isn't Syland (or "Sy")
      because capital letters are determined by the Unicode character encoding…". Same size as the broken model on the
      left, but it kept the experts for code instead of a little of everything.
