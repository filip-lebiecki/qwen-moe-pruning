Chapter 6

Choosing which experts to keep: REAP

"Used often" and "important" are not the same thing. REAP scores an expert by how much it changes the output on the tokens it handles, and in our runs it beat counting usage at every K.

Try it: compare criteria

Illustrative layer with 256 experts, generated so that REAP and frequency agree on 73% of the kept experts at K = 128, as they did on average in the real statistics. Real agreement at K = 48 was 45%.

What to remember

For every token routed to expert j, its contribution to the output is g_j(x) · E_j(x): the routing weight times the expert's output vector. Its size is g_j(x) · ‖E_j(x)‖. REAP averages that over the tokens the expert actually handled:

REAP_j = mean over tokens routed to j of g_j(x) · ‖E_j(x)‖

Removing an expert with a small REAP score changes little, because whenever it fired its weighted contribution was small anyway. Frequency keeps "background" experts that are added to many tokens as a minor ingredient. REAP keeps specialists that dominate the few tokens they handle. Paper: REAP the Experts: Why Pruning Prevails for One-Shot MoE Compression (Cerebras, 2025).
