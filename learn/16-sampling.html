Chapter 16

From probabilities to text: sampling and chat templates

The model never writes text; it outputs a probability for each of 248,320 tokens. A sampler picks one, it is appended, and the model runs again. This page uses the 19 B model's real probabilities to show what temperature, top-k, top-p and min-p do, and how a chat, including "thinking", is turned into one text to continue.

Try it: build a sampler

Computed in your browser from the model's 12 most likely tokens (their real log-probabilities at temperature 1, renormalised among themselves). The other 248,308 tokens are left out here; the animation above shows measured values that include them. Temperature 0 means greedy: always the top token.

What to remember

Temperature divides the logits before the softmax: below 1 the leader pulls away, above 1 everything moves towards equal. Because the vocabulary is huge, a high temperature hands a lot of probability to the long tail: at 1.5, " Canberra" fell from 70% to 19%.

Filters cut the tail. Top-k keeps a fixed number; top-p keeps the likeliest tokens up to a total probability; min-p keeps tokens above a fraction of the top one. Top-p and min-p adapt: one candidate when the model is sure, several when it is not. Qwen recommends temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20 and presence penalty 1.5 in thinking mode.

A chat is one text. The template wraps each message in <|im_start|>role … <|im_end|> and ends with <|im_start|>assistant; the model continues from there and stops by writing <|im_end|>. "Thinking off" is the template pre-filling an empty <think></think>.
