Chapter 4

The router

Each MoE layer has a router: one small 256 x 2048 matrix that scores every expert for the current token. Five steps turn those scores into the 8 experts the token uses and the weights their outputs get.

Try it: what load balancing does

Bars: share of routed tokens per expert in one layer, sorted from most to least used. In our statistics the 128 most-used experts of a layer received 73.7% of the traffic on average. Low imbalance means no expert is useless, so pruning has no free lunch. High imbalance (what a model without a load-balancing loss tends to do) would leave many rarely used experts that could be dropped almost for free.

What to remember

The router computes logits = W · x (one score per expert), then softmax, keeps the top 8, and renormalizes those 8 so they sum to 1. Those are the routing weights g. The layer's output is Σ g_j · E_j(x) + shared(x).

Row j of the router matrix acts as a description of the tokens expert j is good at. When an expert is pruned, its row is deleted too, and the top 8 are picked among the survivors (chapter 5).
