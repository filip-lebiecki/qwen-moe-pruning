Appendix G · inside the experts

What does each expert handle?

Nobody assigns experts a topic; specialities emerge in training. Here they are, measured: which tokens each of the 10,240 experts received when 524k tokens of calibration text went through the original model.

Explore: pick a layer, click an expert

Colour strength = how specialised the expert is (its highest lift). Click one.

What to remember

Lift = share of a token among the expert's tokens ÷ share of that token in all text. It finds what is special about an expert instead of what is common everywhere. Tokens seen fewer than 20 times are ignored.

Specialities are real but partial: experts work in teams of 8 per token, one expert can serve several unrelated jobs, and a token list shows correlation, not necessity. The causal test is to delete the expert and measure, which is what pruning does, and appendix I does it: one named expert alone changes almost nothing, a team of 4 topic experts per layer costs its topic 25–51%. Most named specialists are rarely used, so they sit near the pruning cut: REAP rescues some (biology, layer 20 · 241, from 229th to 2nd), but the 128-expert prune still deleted algebra, code comments and think/LaTeX.
