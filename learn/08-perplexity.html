Perplexity: how we measure damage

Perplexity asks how surprised the model is by real text. At every position we look at the probability it gave to the token that actually came next. Everything here uses real probabilities from your pruned 19B model.

Try it: change the model's confidence

Each slider is the probability of the correct next token at one position (log scale). One very surprising token, like "␣capital" right after "The", raises the average a lot. That is why perplexity punishes confident mistakes and rare words.

What to remember

loss = average of −ln(probability of the correct next token) and perplexity = e^loss. A perplexity of 5 means the model is, on average, as uncertain as if it were picking uniformly among 5 tokens. Lower is better.

We always measure on held-out text (data/eval.pt) that was never used for calibration, split by domain. Perplexity is quick and objective, but it is not the same as answer quality: always test real prompts too (scripts/ask.py). In our runs +20% (K=128) still answered everything correctly, +140% (K=48) produced broken code and repetition loops.
