Does an expert matter? Deleting one, and merging instead of deleting

Appendix G showed which tokens each expert receives. That is correlation. Here each named expert is deleted on its own and the damage is measured token by token, on texts sorted by topic. Then the experts a prune drops are merged into survivors instead of thrown away, to test the REAP paper's claim that deleting is better.

What to remember

Ablation turns correlation into cause. Delete the part, keep everything else, and measure. Measure the unchanged model twice first: here the GPU's atomic additions alone moved perplexity by up to 0.15%, more than one expert's effect, until the sum was made deterministic.

Lift says what an expert is about; share says how much the model relies on it. Experts with the most characteristic tokens often handle well under 1% of a domain's tokens, and deleting them changes almost nothing, because their few tokens re-route to neighbours. An expert that carries a large share of a domain (here, a geometry expert with 47%) does real damage when removed, and exactly on that domain.

Averaging two networks needs aligned neurons. The hidden neurons of an expert can be listed in any order, and a neuron's up row and down column can both flip sign, without changing what it computes. Averaging two experts position by position therefore mixes unrelated neurons; matching them first (the Hungarian algorithm) fixes that part. It cannot fix that two different experts compute different things.
