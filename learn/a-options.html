Appendix A

Ways to shrink a model

This project started with "shrink Qwen3.8-27B to 9B". There are five different ways to make a model smaller, and they differ in what they remove, whether the model must be retrained afterwards, and what that costs. This page explains why we ended up deleting experts from an MoE model.

What to remember

Quantization and pruning answer different questions. Quantization keeps every parameter and stores it more compactly; a 27 B model stays a 27 B model. Pruning removes parameters, so the parameter count really drops.

Cutting layers or narrowing matrices of a dense model damages every token's computation, so the model must be retrained (distilled) on tens of billions of tokens to recover. Deleting experts from an MoE model only affects the tokens that used them, and the router reroutes those to their next choices, which is why a moderate prune works without retraining. Experts can also be scored one layer at a time, which is what made this project fit on one GPU.

The two can also be compared head to head. In step 4 the original 35 B, quantized to 2.6 bits per weight, was the same 10.6 GiB file as the pruned 19 B at 4.9 bits, and it scored 91.4 against 82.7 on the benchmarks (appendix H). Quantization blurs every weight a little; pruning deletes half the experts outright. Try quantization alone first, and prune when even its smallest usable file is too big.
